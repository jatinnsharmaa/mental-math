# Exponent Rules

12 rules for manipulating powers — formula, what it means, a worked example, and a mental picture to anchor it.

| # | Rule | Formula | Meaning | Example | Picture in mind |
|---|------|---------|---------|---------|------------------|
| 1 | Product | `a^m · a^n = a^(m+n)` | Multiplying means stacking the same base's factors together. | 2³ · 2⁴ = 2⁷ | Lego bricks — a stack of 3 plus a stack of 4 → one stack of 7. |
| 2 | Quotient | `a^m / a^n = a^(m-n)` | Division means cancelling common factors. | 5⁶/5² = 5⁴ | Cross out matching bricks from the top and bottom stacks. |
| 3 | Power of a Power | `(a^m)^n = a^(mn)` | Repeating a power multiplies the exponents. | (3²)⁴ = 3⁸ | A box of boxes — 4 boxes, 2 items each → 8 items total. |
| 4 | Power of a Product | `(ab)^n = a^n · b^n` | The power applies to every factor inside. | (2·5)³ = 2³·5³ = 1000 | Scaling a recipe to serve more people — every ingredient scales up equally. |
| 5 | Power of a Fraction | `(a/b)^n = a^n / b^n` | The exponent applies to both numerator and denominator. | (3/4)² = 9/16 | Shrinking both the top and bottom of a photo equally. |
| 6 | Zero Exponent | `a^0 = 1` | Anything divided by itself is 1. | 7⁵/7⁵ = 7⁰ = 1 | A building with 0 floors still has the ground floor — "1." |
| 7 | Negative Exponent | `a^(-n) = 1/a^n` | Negative means "flip to the other side of the fraction." | 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8 | Inverting a fraction, like flipping a pancake. |
| 8 | Fractional Exponent | `a^(m/n) = ⁿ√(a^m)` | The denominator is the root, the numerator is the power. | 27^(2/3) = (∛27)² = 9 | First unpack into the smaller root pieces, then square them. |
| 9 | Factor Smaller Power (+) | `a^m + a^n = a^min(m,n)(a^\|m-n\| + 1)` | Pull out the smaller stack to simplify. | 2⁵ + 2³ = 2³(2²+1) = 40 | Factor out the common bricks, then see what's left. |
| 10 | Factor Smaller Power (−) | `a^m − a^n = a^min(m,n)(a^\|m-n\| − 1)` | Same as addition, just a minus inside. | 3⁷ − 3⁵ = 3⁵(3²−1) = 1944 | Pull out the smaller pile, look at the leftover difference. |
| 11 | Negative Exponents Factoring | `a^(-p) + a^(-q) = a^min(-p,-q)(a^\|(-p)-(-q)\| + 1)` | Pick the "more negative" exponent to factor out. | 2⁻² + 2⁻⁵ = 2⁻⁵(2³+1) = 9/32 | Factor out the smaller (more negative) base, then simplify. |
| 12 | Growth/Decay Pattern | `Value = Initial × r^t` | Multiply by the same factor repeatedly for each step of growth or shrinkage. | 100 grows 10%/yr → 100·(1.1)^t | A snowball getting bigger each time you roll it. |
